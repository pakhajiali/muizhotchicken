From Food to Coffee to Parcel Collection: Building a Multi-Service Business

Three services. One address. How Restoran Pak Haji Ali & Muiz Hot Chicken - Subang Jaya (USJ 8) built a business that stays busy every single day of the week.

4 October 2026 7 min read USJ 8, Subang Jaya
Most restaurants sell one thing: food. When people are hungry, you're busy. When they're not, you're not. That's the trap of the single-service business.

Restoran Pak Haji Ali & Muiz Hot Chicken - Subang Jaya (USJ 8) deliberately built a multi-service business instead. Food brings in one kind of visit. Coffee brings in another. Parcel collection brings in a third — and it happens every single day, hungry or not.

Service 02

Coffee — The Reason to Stop In Between Meals

Fills the 3 PM slump

A restaurant that only sells meals has two dead zones: mid-afternoon and late evening. Coffee combos close that gap. Restoran Pak Haji Ali & Muiz Hot Chicken - Subang Jaya (USJ 8) partnered with ZUS Coffee to bundle a proper cup of coffee with its food — creating a reason to visit that isn't a full meal.

Original Regular Box + ZUS Coffee — RM13.00. Two pieces of Muiz Hot Chicken plus a ZUS Coffee (White or Chocolate). Saves RM0.90 versus buying separately — and it turns a snack into a full stop.
A natural office lunch. The coffee combo is priced for corporate lunch orders — premium enough for a client meeting, affordable enough for a regular workday.
A different customer. Coffee attracts the mid-afternoon crowd, the study crowd and the "just need a break" crowd — people who wouldn't have come for a full meal.
Why it matters: a meal-only business earns during meal times. A business that also sells coffee earns during every waking hour — from the same counter, with the same staff.

Why Multi-Service Beats Single-Service

Three structural advantages that a single-service restaurant simply cannot match.

Advantage 01

Higher Visit Frequency

A single-service restaurant sees each customer maybe once every two weeks. A multi-service business sees the same customer three to five times a week — once for a parcel, once for lunch, once for coffee. Every extra visit is another chance to sell something.

Advantage 02

Revenue That Doesn't Rise and Fall Together

When it rains, dine-in drops — but delivery spikes. When it's a quiet weekday, food slows — but parcels still arrive. When lunch is over, meals stop — but coffee keeps the counter busy. Three services mean three different rhythms, and they rarely all go quiet at the same time.

Advantage 03

Lower Cost Per Customer

A single-service restaurant pays to acquire every customer — through ads, discounts, or driving them across town. A multi-service business gets a share of its traffic for free, because the parcel companies are already paying to send people to the door. That's the same customer, acquired at zero marketing cost.

None of these services would work nearly as well on their own.

1

2

4

The key insight: the parcel counter doesn't just bring in parcel collectors. It also brings in the conditions that make food, coffee and local search work far better than they would alone.

What It Actually Takes to Run Three Services

Multi-service sounds simple. It isn't. These are the four operational requirements.

Requirement 01

Staff Who Can Switch Roles

The same counter handles food orders, coffee combos and parcel collections. That requires staff who can move between three workflows without slowing anyone down — and a queue system that doesn't let parcel pickups block the lunch rush.

Requirement 02

Physical Space for Parcels

Parcel collection takes up real floor space. A dedicated shelf area — clearly signed and away from the dining area — is essential. Without it, the restaurant turns into a warehouse and the dining experience suffers.

Requirement 03

Clean Accounting for Each Service

Three services means three revenue streams — and three sets of costs. Food costs are tracked separately from parcel handling. Coffee combos have their own margin profile. Without clean accounting, you can't tell which service is actually pulling its weight.

Requirement 04

Standards That Don't Slip

JAKIM Halal and KKM MeSTI certifications don't just cover the food. The entire premises — including the parcel area — has to meet the standard. That's the trade-off: multi-service is harder to run, but it's also harder for a competitor to copy.

The Multi-Service Playbook

1
Start with food — but don't stop there Food is the foundation. It earns the right to add more services on top.
2
Add coffee to fill the dead hours Coffee combos capture customers between meal times — and give corporate clients a premium option.
3
Add parcel collection to guarantee daily traffic A logistics counter brings in visits that no amount of advertising can buy.
4
Layer the services so each one feeds the next Parcel visits → food conversions → coffee add-ons → reviews → higher search ranking.
5
Run it like three businesses, not one Separate staff flows, separate shelves, separate accounting — one kitchen, one standard.
