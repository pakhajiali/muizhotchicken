The Core Idea

A dine-in restaurant is a fixed asset. A catering business is a mobile one.

The Limitation

A Restaurant Can Only Serve Who Walks In

A dine-in restaurant in Goodyear Court 6 can only serve the people who physically arrive at Goodyear Court 6. That's fine for the neighbourhood — but it means the business is capped by geography.

Fixed seating = fixed revenue. Whether the shop seats 40 or 140, there is a hard maximum on how many meals can be sold per lunch hour.
Fixed location = fixed audience. Customers have to come to you — which means anyone more than a short drive away simply never becomes a customer.
The question that started Pillar 1: why should the kitchen's output be limited by the size of the dining room?
The Vision

A Kitchen With No Walls

A catering order can take Restoran Pak Haji Ali & Muiz Hot Chicken - Subang Jaya (USJ 8) to places the restaurant itself could never reach. The same JAKIM Halal certified kitchen that serves walk-ins in USJ 8 can just as easily serve 300 people at a seminar in Shah Alam, a school event in Puchong, or a wedding in Klang.

The mission is simple:

1
Take the food out of the restaurant — and put it wherever the crowd already is.
2
Replace tables with trays. One delivery van replaces an entire dining hall.
3
Serve Selangor, not just USJ 8. The kitchen stays. The map expands.
Why this is Pillar 1: every other expansion idea — more outlets, more services, more channels — depends on the business first proving it can produce and deliver reliably at volume. Catering is that proof.

Why the Kitchen Can Already Handle This

Catering isn't a new business. It's the same business, applied to a bigger room.

Foundation 01

JAKIM Halal + KKM MeSTI Certified

Most institutional catering buyers — schools, government departments, corporate procurement teams — will not sign off on a caterer without proper certification. Restoran Pak Haji Ali & Muiz Hot Chicken - Subang Jaya (USJ 8) already holds both JAKIM Halal and KKM MeSTI certifications, which means it clears the first screening stage automatically.

Foundation 02

A Kitchen Built for Volume

The USJ 8 kitchen already produces at scale for daily dine-in, delivery and parcel-hour rush. Adding a catering batch of 300 boxed meals doesn't require new equipment — it requires a scheduled production block and a delivery run.

How a Catering Order Actually Works

From first message to final delivery — five steps, no surprises.

Process

2
Receive the menu proposal. A recommended menu and per-pax quote within 1 working day.
3
Confirm and deposit. A 50% deposit locks in the date and production slot.

What the Mission Actually Means

Not just more orders. A different kind of business.

Meaning 01

Revenue Without More Tables

A single 300-pax catering order is worth as much as several full days of dine-in lunch service. And it doesn't require a single additional chair, table, or parking space.

Meaning 02

Predictable, Scheduled Revenue

Dine-in is unpredictable — it depends on weather, traffic, mood and competition. Catering is booked in advance. A confirmed order for next Thursday is revenue the business can plan around.

A Brand That Travels

Every catering delivery is a physical advertisement. Hundreds of people at a corporate event in Shah Alam, a school function in Puchong or a wedding in Klang experience the food directly — and most of them have never been to USJ 8.

Corporate clients rebook. Schools hold events every term. Government departments run programmes every quarter. A single successful catering order is not a one-off — it's the start of a recurring relationship.
